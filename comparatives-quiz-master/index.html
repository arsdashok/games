Teacher Guide: The Unpopular Opinions Show

Student: Alina/Player (Preply, A2)  |  Time: ~20 min  |  Topic: Comparative & superlative adjectives

Pain point: 2-syllable adjectives: when -er/-est vs more/most. She knows the short/long rule but can't apply it under pressure, gets frustrated.

Vibe: NO drilling, NO "fill the gap". This is a TALK SHOW. She's the host, you're the guest (or vice versa). She produces sentences naturally while chatting. Correct gently by echoing the right form, don't stop the flow.

Structure:

1. Slide 1 (Title) - introduce the show, assign roles (2 min)

2. Round 1: Quick Fire - she sees adjective, says comparative + superlative. Fun buzzer energy (4 min)

3. Round 2: Unpopular Opinions Debate - she makes wild comparative claims, you argue back (8 min)

4. Round 3: The Awards Ceremony - she presents awards using superlatives (6 min)

5. Round 4: Quiz Master (the big one, 15+ min) - SHE is the host. She reads each prompt, puts the adjective into the right form, and asks YOU the trivia question. You answer (be wrong sometimes, it's funnier). She checks you with the Answer Card and awards/removes points. The Answer Card has FACTS ONLY (no grammar forms) on purpose: she must produce the comparative/superlative herself before asking. Don't rush: let her wrestle the 2-syllable ones, hint verbally with the -y trick if she's stuck.

Quiz tip: the trivia is loaded with 1-syllable easies (confidence) plus the tricky split: -y → -ier/-iest (heavier, busier, busiest, heaviest) vs more/most (more expensive, more dangerous, most popular) plus one irregular (better). UAE hooks: tallest building = Burj Khalifa, busiest airport = Dubai.

Part 3 (Q21-25) is her exact weak spot: 2-syllable words that look short but take more / the most (boring, modern, famous, crowded, careful) NOT -er/-est. The whole point: "boring → more boring", never "boringer". Q24 and Q25 are opinion questions (no fact to check), so it's pure chat plus form practice, which she enjoys.

Correction style: If she says "more funny", just naturally repeat "funnier, yes!" and keep going. If she self-corrects, celebrate it. Never say "wrong".

Key rule to sneak in: 2-syllable + ends in -y = change to -ier/-iest (busy, funny, easy, happy). Other 2-syllable = more/most (boring, stylish, famous).

Round 2: Unpopular Opinions! 🤯

Make a CRAZY comparison. The other person argues back!
How to play
Pick two things. Say something NOBODY agrees with. Use a comparative.
The other person says "No way!" and argues back with a comparative or superlative.

Example:

🎙️ "I think homework is more exciting than parties!"
🗣️ "No way! Parties are the most exciting thing in the world! Homework is more boring than watching paint dry!"
🎙️ "But doing homework makes you smarter! Being smart is better than dancing!"

Round 3: The Awards Ceremony! 🏆

You are the presenter. Give the award and explain WHY!
How to play
Read the award category. Decide who/what wins. Say: "And the award goes to... [name]! Because they are the [superlative]!"
Then explain in 2-3 sentences using comparatives.

Example:

🏆 The Most Boring Film: "And the award goes to... that maths documentary! It was more boring than watching my cat sleep. My cat is funnier than that film. It was the worst film I have ever seen!"

Remember: -y words = -iest (funniest, busiest). Long words = the most (the most dangerous, the most expensive). Irregulars: the best, the worst.

Round 4: The 2-Syllable Trap! ⚠️

Part 3: short-looking words that STILL need more / the most
Watch out Some 2-syllable words look short but still take more / the most: boring, modern, famous, careful, crowded, helpful, nervous, foolish, tiring, stylish, honest, pleasant. We say more boring, never "boringer"! Only 2-syllable words ending in -y take -ier/-iest (busy → busier).
