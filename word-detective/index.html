Game · Word Detective

Detective · drilling «What does it mean?» + «How do you spell it?»
10 min
Setup · for Dasha Goal: Detective must reflexively use the two phrases (What does it mean? / How do you spell it?) when she hears a word she doesn't know.
How: read each round's paragraph aloud at normal speed. The yellow-highlighted words are deliberate «unknowns». Detective interrupts each one with «What does it mean?» → you give the simple definition (below the paragraph) → she asks «How do you spell it?» → you spell it letter-by-letter → she writes it in the word list at the bottom. If she forgets to ask either phrase, just wait silently — don't move on until she produces the phrase.
Three rounds + reverse round. ~10 min total.
Round 1 · Galápagos trip 3 words
Read aloud «Your itinerary for the Galápagos starts in Quito. You'll fly to the islands, where the altitude at sea level keeps things easy on your body. The wildlife is mostly endemic — you won't see those animals anywhere else on the planet.»
Definitions (give simply when asked)
itinerary /aɪˈtɪn.ər.i/
your travel plan — day-by-day list of where you go and when
altitude /ˈæl.tɪ.tjuːd/
height above sea level
endemic /enˈdem.ɪk/
only found in one place — nowhere else in the world
Round 2 · February cheese expo 3 words
Read aloud «Every February you're an exhibitor at the Moscow cheese expo. Most of your buyers are wholesalers — they take big quantities, not one box. You sell yourself as an artisan producer, not factory.»
Definitions
exhibitor /ɪɡˈzɪb.ɪ.tər/
a person or company that shows products at an exhibition / expo
wholesaler /ˈhəʊl.seɪ.lər/
a buyer who takes big quantities and resells to shops
artisan /ˈɑː.tɪ.zæn/
someone who makes things by hand in small batches — opposite of factory
Round 3 · British summer weekend 3 words
Read aloud «In England a fortnight of warm weather is rare, so people make plans fast. Every village has its summer fête — bunting, cakes, a tombola. And don't leave home without a brolly, just in case.»
Definitions
fortnight /ˈfɔːt.naɪt/
two weeks (very common in British English)
fête /feɪt/
a village fair — cakes, games, stalls, often raises money for a school or church
brolly /ˈbrɒl.i/
informal British word for umbrella
Reverse round · Detective = expert, Dasha = curious tourist
Now flip roles. Detective describes one cheese she sells (e.g. Camembert, Brie, Cheddar, Parmesan) in 2-3 English sentences, deliberately using cheese vocabulary you suspect Dasha won't know (rind, mould, ageing, raw milk, hard / soft, runny, crumbly). Dasha asks What does it mean? and How do you spell it? for each tricky word — Detective has to define + spell. Reverses the production direction so she practises GIVING the definition + the spelling, not just receiving.
